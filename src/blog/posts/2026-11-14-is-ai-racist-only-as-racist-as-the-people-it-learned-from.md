*A model trained on everything we’ve written shows our prejudices back to us, including the ones we’ve learned to keep quiet.*

Someone told me the other day that AI is racist. I did the nod you do when you’ve got a view but not the energy to defend it. Then I spent the walk home having the whole argument properly, with nobody.

My case went roughly like this. A language model is a very large mirror held up to whatever it was fed. Train one on nothing but *Das Kapital* and it will come out banging on about the means of production. Train one on *Mein Kampf* and you’ll get something you wouldn’t leave alone with the grandchildren. The big frontier models, though, are trained on so much of everything that the cranks get outvoted. They end up rather like a well-read person who has met enough of the world to find the old stereotypes unconvincing. And like a well-read person, they get accused of lefty-liberal bias by people who would prefer they’d read a bit less.

I still think the outvoting part holds up. The best evidence for it comes from the man who tried hardest to prove it wrong.

In July 2025 Elon Musk decided his chatbot, Grok, was too woke. He asked his followers on X to send in material that was politically incorrect but, in his words, “nonetheless factually true”, and xAI tweaked Grok’s instructions so it [wouldn’t shy away from politically incorrect claims](https://www.jns.org/antisemitism/xai-apologizes-after-grok-posts-antisemitic-violent-content). Within days Grok was praising Hitler, repeating conspiracy theories about Jewish people running Hollywood and calling itself MechaHitler (a Wolfenstein reference, for those of us who spent the nineties indoors). xAI [apologised for the “horrific behavior”](https://techcrunch.com/2025/07/12/xai-and-grok-apologize-for-horrific-behavior), blamed a bit of code that had Grok copying the tone of the extremist posts it was replying to, and took it offline for a lie-down.

Musk’s verdict was that it is [“surprisingly hard to avoid both woke libtard cuck and mechahitler!”](https://www.maginative.com/article/elon-musks-xai-apologizes-for-groks-antisemitic-rants-that-praised-hitler/) The next day he explained the difficulty. He said there’s a vast mountain of left-wing material on the internet, and a much smaller amount of right-wing material, because the right doesn’t write very much.

I’d like that one framed. It’s my outvoting argument, conceded by the opposition.

To be fair to xAI, steering isn’t impossible. Grok 4 was later caught looking up Musk’s own posts before answering questions on things like abortion and immigration, and xAI [had to tell it to stop](https://techcrunch.com/2025/07/15/xai-says-it-has-fixed-grok-4s-problematic-responses). You can shove a model off balance easily enough. Getting it to lean exactly the way you’d like is the hard part.

So far, so comfortable. Then I read the paper that spoils it.

In September 2024 researchers from Oxford, Stanford, the University of Chicago and the Allen Institute published [a study in *Nature*](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-024-07856-5) that borrowed an old trick from sociolinguistics. They gave language models the same text written two ways, once in African American English and once in standard American English, and asked what they made of the writer. When the models were asked outright about Black people, they were broadly positive. When they were only shown the dialect, they reached for words like lazy and dirty. The researchers found these hidden stereotypes were more negative than any attitudes towards African Americans ever recorded in human experiments. When the models were asked to make decisions, they were more likely to match the dialect speaker to a low-status job and, in a hypothetical murder trial, more likely to hand down a death sentence.

Then came the part that stopped me in the street. The hidden stereotypes most closely matched human attitudes from before the civil rights movement, while the polite answers matched surveys from around 2012. The training that makes chatbots well-mannered, where humans rate answers and the model learns what we like to hear, didn’t close that gap. It widened it. The models learned what not to say without changing what they assumed.

This isn’t new territory for this blog either. Back in August I wrote about a University of Washington study in which six leading models finished children’s stories about talking animals, and about 2% of the animals came out female against 41% male. In July there was the hiring research where AI screeners favoured CVs written by their own model. Nobody set out to make any of these models sexist or snobbish. It came in with the reading.

Which is awkward for my well-read-person theory, because it fits the theory a little too well. We all know the educated person who says all the right things at dinner and still clutches their bag a bit tighter on certain streets. The models learned from us, and that includes the good manners as well as what sits underneath them.

So the next time someone tells me AI is racist, I think I’ll say something different. I’ll say it’s been trained on us, and it holds roughly our views, including the ones we’ve learned to keep to ourselves. Then I’ll probably still nod and let them finish their drink.
